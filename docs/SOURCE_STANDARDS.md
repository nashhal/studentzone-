# Yemen Study — source and research standards

Last reviewed: 19 November 2026

## 1. Purpose

Yemen Study will cover the Yemeni school levels from Grade 1 Basic Education through Grade 3 Secondary. The Yemeni curriculum determines **what is taught**. Reliable international references help explain concepts, check scientific facts, verify terminology and inspire original teaching approaches. An international source does not prove that a topic belongs in a Yemeni grade or edition.

Every published lesson must identify its grade, track where relevant, subject, unit, syllabus year/edition, regional scope, and the sources used to verify it.

## 2. Source hierarchy

1. **Curriculum authority:** the relevant Yemeni Ministry of Education / General Administration of E-Learning release for the exact school year and edition. Record the textbook title, cover/edition, table-of-contents location and relevant page range. The Ministry's e-learning portal is the primary starting point: https://e-learning-moe.edu.ye/book.php
2. **Regional confirmation:** education-office catalogues can help locate files, but must be labelled by governorate. For example, the Hadramout Education Office catalogue is regional and is not, by itself, proof that a syllabus applies to every governorate: https://meoh.gov.ye/services/subjects/
3. **International subject references:** use reputable universities, scientific bodies, standards institutions and public agencies to independently check explanations, terminology, formulae and examples.
4. **Teacher review:** a subject teacher checks grade suitability, alignment, clarity, language and every worked answer before publication.

A source may be authoritative for a scientific fact but not for the Yemeni syllabus. Keep these two questions separate.

## 3. Recommended international reference catalogue

The default rule is **reference and cite, write independently**. Do not assume that free-to-read means free-to-copy.

### Important licence and terms checks

- **OpenStax:** its textbook content is published under Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0 (CC BY-NC-SA). Reuse/adaptation requires attribution, non-commercial use and sharing adaptations under the same licence. If Yemen Study later becomes ad-supported, paid or otherwise commercial, do not rely on this licence for reuse without permission. https://help.openstax.org/s/article/Licensing-information-of-OpenStax-textbooks
- **PhET:** regular HTML simulations are CC BY-NC 4.0. Commercial use, including ad-supported or paid products, requires a separate licence. Attribution must be close to the simulation and the PhET logo must remain visible and unaltered. https://phet.colorado.edu/en/licensing/
- **MIT OpenCourseWare:** materials are available under CC BY-NC-SA terms; use is non-commercial, attributed to MIT and shared alike. Suitable mainly for teacher enrichment and advanced secondary explanations, not as a substitute for the local syllabus. https://mitocw.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/5093047407771-Reusing-MIT-OpenCourseWare-Content
- **IUPAC Gold Book:** individual terms are licensed CC BY-SA 4.0. The site notes that some definitions may be outdated or superseded; verify the cited source and current scientific consensus before using a term. https://goldbook.iupac.org/
- **Khan Academy:** do not scrape, bulk-copy, translate, re-host or use its exercise/content data to build Yemen Study lessons. Its current terms explicitly restrict scraping and reproduction/derivative use. Use only as an independent external learning destination where appropriate. https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/42929097425037-What-s-allowed-and-not-allowed-under-Khan-Academy-s-Terms-of-Service
- **CK-12:** do not aggregate or distribute its curriculum materials inside Yemen Study without express written permission. Its terms specifically restrict content aggregators from obtaining materials for distribution through their own services. https://help.ck12.org/hc/en-us/articles/51042851054235-CK-12-Terms-of-Use
- **Wolfram MathWorld:** useful as a mathematical reference, but its terms prohibit bulk copying, mirroring and automated archival copying. https://www.wolfram.com/legal/terms/mathworld/
- **UNESCO OER:** a resource is an Open Educational Resource only when it is public domain or under an open licence that permits reuse, adaptation and redistribution. Being online or free to read is not enough. https://www.unesco.org/en/open-educational-resources

This catalogue is a starting register, not blanket permission. The exact resource, image, diagram, dataset or chapter must be checked before copying or embedding it.

## 4. Original lesson and problem-writing rules

### Explanations

- Write in original Arabic appropriate to the target grade. Define technical terms and give the English term in parentheses only when useful.
- Explain one learning objective at a time. Move from familiar examples to abstract concepts and then to formal notation.
- Use international sources to check correctness, not to translate or paraphrase a source paragraph sentence by sentence.
- Separate the core required explanation from optional enrichment. Mark advanced content clearly.
- Cite the source title, author/publisher, exact chapter/section or page, URL, access date and the claim it was used to verify.

### Worked examples and questions

- Write new questions with original contexts, values, diagrams and wording. Do not copy end-of-chapter questions, answer choices, worked solutions or question-bank datasets from websites.
- For each numerical problem, record the givens, required quantity, formula, unit conversion, substitutions, calculation, final answer and reasonableness check.
- Check dimensions, units, signs, rounding/significant figures and alternate valid methods where relevant.
- Questions must test a stated learning objective and have a difficulty tag: easy, medium or advanced.
- Every published answer must include a teaching explanation, not only the correct option.
- A second reviewer should independently recalculate science and mathematics problems before publication.

## 5. Publication gate

Use the editorial sequence: **draft → review → published**.

A lesson can be published only when all are true:

- Its grade/track/subject/unit is mapped to an identified Yemeni syllabus version and regional scope.
- Its objectives and lesson order have been checked against the relevant syllabus source.
- Its explanations, terms, formulae and units have reliable source references.
- Examples and question sets are original or explicitly licensed for reuse.
- All answers and explanations have been independently checked.
- Any image, diagram, dataset, simulation or quotation has a recorded rights decision and attribution.
- A qualified subject reviewer has approved the lesson.

When the correct edition cannot be confirmed, keep the item as a draft or label it as an unverified sample. Never claim ministry approval or current-edition alignment without evidence.

## 6. Source record fields

For every source, record:

- Title, author/organisation, publisher and URL.
- Source type (curriculum authority, textbook, open educational resource, scientific reference, primary source, interactive tool or dataset).
- Publication year, exact edition, chapter/section/page or data identifier.
- Licence name and licence URL, if stated.
- Reuse policy: link only, reference only, reuse permitted, non-commercial reuse only, public domain verified, or permission required.
- Date checked, who checked it and notes on limitations.

For every lesson/question, link all supporting sources and state their role: curriculum alignment, fact check, explanation reference, worked-example reference, visual/media, or further reading.

## 7. Coverage and delivery

The coverage matrix in [CURRICULUM_COVERAGE.md](./CURRICULUM_COVERAGE.md) is the single progress register. Do not count a subject tile or a source link as a completed lesson. Count only teacher-reviewed and published lessons. Build by verified syllabus units, not by inventing a target number of lessons.
