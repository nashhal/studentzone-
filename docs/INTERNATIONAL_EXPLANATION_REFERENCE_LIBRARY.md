# Yemen Study — International Explanation Reference Library

Last reviewed: 20 November 2026

## Editorial promise

The Yemeni 2026–2027 curriculum plan supplied for this project determines **which lessons are included and in what order**. International educational websites are used to improve the **quality of explanations**, check scientific and mathematical accuracy, find clearer ways to teach difficult concepts, and identify suitable visual or interactive demonstrations.

Yemen Study explanations are written in original Arabic. The workflow is **study the reference → verify the concept with a second reliable source → explain it afresh in Arabic → create new examples and questions → cite the references**.

This is not a machine translation or a line-by-line rewrite of a website, video transcript, exercise bank, image caption, or textbook. Copyrighted lessons, diagrams, videos, quizzes, and answer keys must not be copied or translated for republication without permission. A link or a free-to-read page is not a reuse licence.

## Use by school stage

### Grades 1–3
Use the official plan to preserve the prescribed subject, sequence and time allocation. International references are supplementary and should be used sparingly: simple mathematics models, child-appropriate science explanations, early English language examples and visual geography. Keep vocabulary and tasks within the reading level of the grade. The official Arabic-language and religious curriculum remains the controlling source for those subjects.

### Grades 4–9
Use BBC Bitesize, Khan Academy and open reference texts to compare teaching sequences and identify common misconceptions. For mathematics and science, develop original worked examples and practice. For English, use British Council as a language-teaching reference. For geography and history, use National Geographic, Britannica and UNESCO for fact-checking, while keeping the local syllabus as the course map.

### Grades 10–12
Use OpenStax, NIST, IUPAC, NCBI, NASA, NOAA and PhET as the main international fact-checking/reference pool for science and mathematics. Use BBC Bitesize and Khan Academy to compare explanations and difficulty progression, not as substitutes for the Yemeni lesson sequence. Use National Geographic, Britannica and UNESCO for relevant geography/history/social-science context. Mark any college-level extension as enrichment, separate from examinable syllabus content.

## Arabic and local-subject safeguards

For Arabic grammar, rhetoric, literature and texts, Qur'an, Hadith, Fiqh, Seerah, Yemeni history and national/civic subjects:

- Preserve the title and scope from the official Yemeni plan and the relevant approved textbook.
- Use trusted Arabic primary/reference works and a qualified subject reviewer.
- Do not replace local texts, interpretations, terminology or learning outcomes with an international curriculum.
- Do not invent quotations, poem lines, hadith attributions, dates or doctrinal positions.
- For protected literary texts, teach context, analysis and language skills without reproducing passages beyond what is legally permitted.

## Lesson authoring workflow

For every official lesson entry:

1. **Curriculum anchor:** record grade, stream, subject, unit, topic/lesson title, school year, source file/page and any official notes.
2. **Reference search:** locate at least one relevant reputable explanation and, for factual/scientific claims, a second independent reference.
3. **Concept extraction:** record only the concepts, terminology, rules and common misconceptions needed for the Yemeni lesson.
4. **Arabic authoring:** explain in clear, accurate Arabic using original wording. Add the English technical term in parentheses only where useful.
5. **Original pedagogy:** write fresh local examples, worked problems, diagrams, practice questions and feedback. Do not translate another site's exercises or solutions.
6. **Alignment check:** ensure the explanation neither omits a listed topic nor quietly adds off-syllabus examinable material. Label useful extensions as enrichment.
7. **Verification:** check formulae, units, dates, definitions, answer keys and age/grade appropriateness; independently recalculate numerical questions.
8. **Source and rights record:** store the exact title, section/page, URL, access date, role, licence/reuse status and attribution requirement.
9. **Review and release:** a subject reviewer approves the batch before it is marked published.

## Required lesson record

Each lesson should store:

- Official curriculum title and exact source/page.
- Original Arabic explanation and learning objectives.
- Key terms (Arabic and English where appropriate).
- Formulae/rules with units and conditions.
- Original worked examples with full reasoning.
- Original practice and answers with explanatory feedback.
- Common misconceptions and a short revision summary.
- References grouped by role: curriculum alignment, fact check, explanation reference, media/simulation and enrichment.
- Rights decision for every borrowed image, diagram, quotation, dataset or interactive component.
- Review status: draft, checked, approved, published.

## Important separation

- **Curriculum source:** the user's Ministry of Education 2026–2027 course-plan PDFs; controls what is included.
- **International references:** improve accuracy and explanation quality; they do not change the official lesson list.
- **Yemen Study content:** original Arabic teaching material authored for this platform; it is not a translated copy of any external website.
- **Student experience:** explanations, worked examples, practice, feedback and summaries must appear inside Yemen Study. External links are optional citations, never a required step to finish a lesson.
